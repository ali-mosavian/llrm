# Optimization levels

TL;DR: what each `-O` level is, and for each limit its gcc and LLVM counterpart with the source line. `-Omax` is every pass on with LLVM's -O3 budgets; `-O0..-O3` are gcc's definitions of the passes this compiler has.

## Passes by level

`x`: the pass does not run. `on`: it runs, with no level-dependent budget. Else its budget at that level. Names are those of `LLRM_DEBUG=time` (`mir`, `lir`) and of `LLRM_DEBUG=runs` (the interprocedural stages). `Level::options` ([`driver/flags.rs`](https://github.com/ali-mosavian/llrm/blob/c341bd6e3de058142c6652d399b8dfeb444eff9b/crates/backend/llrm-core/src/driver/flags.rs#L51)) maps -Og to -O1's less `loopidioms`; under `llrm-cc` -Og is clang's -O1 (`LoopIdiomRecognize` included), so the less-`loopidioms` rule applied to llrm-c only; the pass options are `pipeline::Options` ([`pipeline.rs`](../optimizations/passes/pipeline.md)). `mir equalcalls` and `mir gvn` share the `gcse` switch. Read from `pipeline.rs` and the backend profile (`size`, `search`, `exhaustive`, `routes`); `LLRM_DEBUG=time` and `runs` print what ran at a level.

Unroll and peel budgets are the target's `unroll_budget` (m32 150, m16 200) times `target_percent` (100; -O3 and -Omax 200). -O3's 300 departs from gcc's 200 (`max-completely-peeled-insns`) to clang's [`UnrollThresholdAggressive`](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/llvmorg-20.1.8/llvm/lib/Transforms/Scalar/LoopUnrollPass.cpp#L201), by the user's decision of 2026-10-11, on the 66 programs at -O3 (model clocks, 150 -> 300): geomean vs gcc 1.0193 -> 0.9828, vs clang 0.8814 -> 0.8499, worst row matmul 8.27 -> hanoi 3.60; five programs change (matmul 10,399 -> 1,189 clocks; crc, fpbench, nbody_single, x_nbody_f 4-10% faster), code of the 66 +16% (28,099 -> 32,695 B), compile instructions +14% on the programs (all from those five) and +0.29% on QCport (65 modules, one changes). The trip cap is lifted from 16 to 64 at -Omax only, by the user's decision of 2026-10-15 (-O1 to -O3 stay gcc's: neither gcc nor clang's cap-free rule is taken there), on the 71 programs, model clocks, -Omax with the cap 16 -> 64: six programs change (tile 8,159 -> 4,182, x_transpose 108,398 -> 81,297, mandel 672,846 -> 552,769, x_bigadd 296,933 -> 245,484, shellsort 12,421 -> 10,860, recchop 6,248 -> 5,941), geomean clocks 0.9787, code of the 71 +23.9% (33,564 -> 41,574 B); d_faces compile unchanged (26,393 M instructions at -Omax). Growth at -O2 (gcc's -O3 rule moved down) was priced and not taken: 12 programs change, geomean 0.9667, code +23.6%. -Os and -Oz are not in the table: -Os is -O2's passes, `peel` on, no copy grows the code, `inline` hint and hot bonuses off, 0 clocks per byte; -Oz is -Os less `unroll` and `peel`. Every pass is on at -Omax; `unswitch` and `unroll_jam` were the last two off, turned on by the user's decision of 2026-10-14: -Omax m32, 71 programs, before -> after: only particle changes (instructions 84,141 -> 68,845, memory operands 61,509 -> 36,259, 486 clocks 165,949 -> 100,153, code 230 -> 255 B); geomean clocks 0.9929, worst row 1.0000; compile instructions +0.45% on the other 70, particle +30% (172M -> 225M).

Not rows: the frontend and link steps (`mir runtime`, `mir link`, `mir verify frontend`), the wrappers `mir pipeline` and `candidate first frame`, the pass manager's own (`mir declared`, `interface`, `invalidate`, `outer analyses`), the analyses, and the allocator's inner steps (`regalloc *`, `spill *`, `split *`, `ssa *`, `siblings *`, `intervals *`, `facts *`); each runs wherever its pass does.

Sources read:

- gcc: [`releases/gcc-13.4.0`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/tree/releases/gcc-13.4.0), [`opts.cc`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/opts.cc) and [`params.opt`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/params.opt). The host's `gcc` is 13.4.0, the one measured.
- LLVM: [`llvmorg-24-init-10533-gd1106deb71cc`](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/tree/d1106deb71cc81016406a1917d0508d2df296266). The host's `clang` is 20.1.8, the one measured; a value read at 24-init may differ there.
- `gcc -Q --help=optimizers` reports `-funroll-loops [enabled]` at `-O2`, and it is: not in `opts.cc` but in the x86 backend's own table, [`i386-common.cc` 1829-1832](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/common/config/i386/i386-common.cc#L1829-L1832) (`OPT_LEVELS_2_PLUS_SPEED_ONLY`: `-funroll-loops` with `-munroll-only-small-loops`, so -O2 and -O3, not -Os). The RTL unroller (`loop2_unroll`) then unrolls only an innermost loop of at most `small_unroll_ninsns` instructions, by at most `small_unroll_factor`, whatever it would otherwise do ([`ix86_loop_unroll_adjust`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/config/i386/i386.cc#L24019-L24034)); the 486's costs give 4 and 2 ([`i486_cost`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/config/i386/x86-tune-costs.h#L361-L362)). An unknown trip count gets a prologue of `n & 1` iterations and a loop of two bodies ([`decide_unroll_runtime_iterations`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/loop-unroll.cc#L656), [`unroll_loop_runtime_iterations`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/loop-unroll.cc#L870)). We do not do it: a trial over the 67 programs at -O2 changed three and none of the four gcc unrolls, with no gain in the model clocks, and rectwo's split hot loops (`-ftree-ch`, about 2 trips an entry) ran 1.9% slower, the odd-trip test costing more than the saved test. gcc's size gate counts RTL instructions after combine, which our count-to-zero counters from LSR do not match, and its under-4-trips gate is a profile estimate we do not have.

## The inline threshold is ours

`Threshold::budget` is `clamp(call_reach / 2, 6, 24) * limit / 225`, compared with the callee's MIR operations (`semantic_count`). So 225 and 250 are ratios (250/225 is LLVM's -O3 over -O2) and the budget is 6 to 24 operations at -O2, 26 at -O3 where `call_reach` allows (m32's is 3, m16's 18: 6 and 9 at -O2, 6 and 10 at -O3, 2 and 3 at -O1); LLVM's 225 is cost units of about 5 per instruction plus a call penalty of 25. Calibration, `clang -O2 -Rpass=inline` against `LLRM_DEBUG=inline`, on `x*3+1`, a small loop and a loop over an array: ours 2, 8 and 10 operations; LLVM `cost=-25` (threshold 337), `10` and `10` (threshold 225), each after its bonuses, so no per-instruction scale can be read off them. -O1's 90 is 225 x `early-inlining-insns` 6 / `max-inline-insns-auto` 15 (params.opt:129, 545).

## Register allocation by level: gcc's IRA against ours

gcc's settings ([`toplev.cc`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/toplev.cc), [`ira.cc`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.4.0/gcc/ira.cc)):

| level | `ira_conflicts_p` | `-fira-region` | `-fcaller-saves` |
|---|---|---|---|
| -O0 | off (`fast_allocation`) | one | off |
| -O1 | on | mixed | off |
| -O2, -O3 | on | mixed | on |
| -Os | on | one | on |

We match -O0 only: `Options::none()` has no allocation search, so a function is allocated once, through the allocator alone (compile -51% on QCport `d_faces`, -48% on `d_alias`, bytes +0.02%).

-O1 equals -O2 in gcc's allocator, so there is nothing to match: our -O1 costs more than gcc's because the allocator runs on a larger body, not because of a policy.

-Os does not match. gcc's one region is the allocator without loop-tree regions; the counterpart here is no live-range splitting. Measured on 131 files: compile -35% (`d_faces`) and -20% (`d_alias`), but bytes +0.49% geomean, worst `pl_trace` +5.8%. Splitting earns its bytes in this allocator, so -Os splits.

A function is allocated once below -Omax, as LLVM's greedy allocator and gcc's IRA do. -Omax tries other shapes of a body (`-fallocation-search`) and makes each function by the allocator alone and by the spiller's route, keeping the cheaper (`-fallocation-routes`). The switches are independent and set either anywhere.

# Recursion rows

D10 of [inline cost](../../crates/opt/llrm-transforms/src/inlinecost.rs): a tree-recursive function is copied into itself as gcc's [`recursive_inlining`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.3.0/gcc/ipa-inline.cc#L1645) does, to depth 3 (gcc: 8), 150 `gcc_insns` (gcc: 450, [params.opt](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.3.0/gcc/params.opt#L549)) and the call's frequency over the entry's above 10% ([`want_inline_self_recursive_call_p`](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.3.0/gcc/ipa-inline.cc#L991), [L1067](https://github.com/gcc-mirror/gcc/blob/releases/gcc-13.3.0/gcc/ipa-inline.cc#L1067)), only where its distinct self-calls average one or more per call (a tree), not at -Os. Measured with the installed gcc 13.4.0 binary's `-Q --help=params`; the cited source is 13.3.0 (same defaults).

Where it runs, and why there: the pin runs the function specializer inside IPSCCP, before its inliner ([PassBuilderPipelines.cpp:1228](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/d1106deb71cc81016406a1917d0508d2df296266/llvm/lib/Passes/PassBuilderPipelines.cpp#L1228), the inliner pipeline at [L1331](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/d1106deb71cc81016406a1917d0508d2df296266/llvm/lib/Passes/PassBuilderPipelines.cpp#L1331)), and tail-call elimination in the function simplification pipeline inside it ([L688](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/d1106deb71cc81016406a1917d0508d2df296266/llvm/lib/Passes/PassBuilderPipelines.cpp#L688)). Here ipa-cp's second round is the interprocedural step after the walk, so the copies made in the walk enlarged `chop` before ipa-cp priced its clones (recchop -O3: 4 defines against 12, 6368 clocks against 6209). DEVIATE from the pin's TRE slot: a tree-recursive function is marked in the walk (`tre-deferred`), skips tail-call elimination there, and gets its copies and then its elimination at the end of the interprocedural step (`walk::SelfInline`). Linear recursions keep the pin's order (a tail-recursive callee is a loop before its caller inlines it, #3256).

Cleanup (D10, third part; DEVIATE from the pin; owner w10-instcombine; exit: remove the second Simplify run when D10's admission can run the cleanup in the pin's inline-then-cleanup order, or when the pin-faithful order gives fib/x_fib at or below main's 227171 without it; review 2026-11-30: re-measure fib/x_fib/rectwo clocks, and if neither exit is reachable it becomes a permanent numbered DEVIATE with this evidence restated): a body that got copies runs the Simplify list twice (`walk::SelfInline`). The pin has no such second run: its function simplification pipeline runs once on a function in the CGSCC walk (`NoRerun`, [PassBuilderPipelines.cpp:1047](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/d1106deb71cc81016406a1917d0508d2df296266/llvm/lib/Passes/PassBuilderPipelines.cpp#L1047)), and it never has self-copies to clean (LLVM refuses a recursive callee). gcc cleans the copies with the passes after `pass_ipa_inline` (FRE and PRE run again, `passes.def`). Here the list runs GVN before jump threading, and the copies' paths are joined by the threading, so run once each copy repeated the calls of the copies that dominate it (fib -O2, depth 3: 154398 clocks, 1426 spills, 12 calls; run twice 14802 clocks, 0 spills). Rows pair at -O1/-O2/-O3/-Os: fib and x_fib -O2/-O3 154398 to 14802, frames -15.6%, rectwo -0.3%, nothing else changes, QCport identical. Answers: the rows' fib, x_fib and frames print the same values as gcc and clang at every level (6765, 6765, 616371). fib -O2 is 14802 clocks against gcc's 210636 and clang's 362808: the calls are pure, and sharing them across the copies makes the recursion cheaper than a tree walk, which neither compiler does.

## DEVIATE REC-1: recursive inlining is a flag, off by default

The pin's inliner does not copy a callee that calls itself into its caller (`IsRecursiveCall`, `AllowRecursiveCall` off); llrm's default now refuses it too (REC-2). gcc's `recursive_inlining` and peeling (above) copy it into itself and into its callers, nine levels deep, which is not the pin's behaviour: hanoi's two identical calls became one by CSE, then `bench_hanoi` took nine copies of `hanoi` and a lookup table for `n < 9`, 40 instructions where gcc runs 35,764 and the pin's clang 249,852 (the 2026-10-23 bench table). The three mechanisms (copies of a function in itself, peeling of a recursive callee's own call in the copy, and the deferral of its tail-call elimination until the copies are made) are `-finline-recursive` (`Threshold::recursive`), off by default; `walk::tests::test_the_default_does_not_copy_a_recursive_callee` and `test_only_a_tree_recursion_waits_for_its_tail_call_elimination` pin the default. Owner: w-8 (flag); w-6 owns the rows above. Exit: the pin-faithful order of inlining and cleanup reaches the rows above without the flag, or the user turns it on.

Not covered by the flag: nothing. A call with a constant argument was inlined again on later rounds only because the callee itself was inlined once and its loop unrolled; the callee is refused now (REC-2).

REC-1 update (2026-10-31, w9): the real-mode target takes a bounded recursion without the flag, at speed only: `Tti::recursive_inlining` (m16: depth 2, a recursive body of at most 14 `gcc_insns`, the 150 `gcc_insns` growth budget and the peeling limits unchanged), so the walk prices a recursive callee (`AllowRecursiveCall`, pin: [`IsRecursiveCall`](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/d1106deb71cc81016406a1917d0508d2df296266/llvm/lib/Analysis/InlineCost.cpp#L2551)) only up to that size and `SelfInline` copies it to that depth.
